# Concept art prompt

The active exterior study was made with the built-in image-generation tool as an edit of an earlier fantasy-fruit study. The prompt was:

> Use case: precise-object-edit. Preserve the same eight separate, vivid, richly textured alien fantasy fruit computer-button shapes, pure black background, two-row arrangement, organic 3D sculptural relief, the assigned exterior hues and distinct colored accents. Make the following specific corrections only: (1) On the golden yellow send icon and purple folder, remove nearly all small dark pores and dots. Use intact luscious skin texture plus at most 3 or 4 subtle soft same-hue sun freckles on a single fruit, never clusters that look like seeds, pits, or damaged fruit. (2) The cyan gear should have eight clearly recognizable substantial gear teeth, each with a rounded but noticeably squared-off TIP and distinct notches, rather than looking like a flower; retain the circular central hole. (3) The add-project icon should remain a purple tabbed folder but move its green plus to the LOWER RIGHT so the plus visibly extends a little beyond the bottom and right edges of the folder, creating a distinctive combined outline; do not let it touch any neighboring icon. (4) The red open folder must stay a DIFFERENT silhouette, with its cyan arrow extending far past the folder's right edge and a pure black gap before the pink plus. (5) The violet refresh arrow's broad ridges should trace the whole curve smoothly lengthwise and remain inside the silhouette. (6) Reduce all nearly white, pale, or blown-out highlights; use soft richly colored reflections that retain saturation and show depth. Keep some contrasting broad fruit blushes and some uniform skins, plenty of organic tactile relief, no generic plastic, no dark outlines, no words, labels, letters, numbers, stems, leaves, seed pits, bruises, or logos.

The result is a concept illustration; the prompt alone does not establish that the image satisfies every requested property. See DESIGN.md for the remaining refinements.
